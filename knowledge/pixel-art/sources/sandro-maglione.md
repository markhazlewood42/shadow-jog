# Sandro Maglione: Getting Started with Pixel Art, a Beginner Perspective

Part of the [[projects/shadow-jog/knowledge/pixel-art/README|pixel-art library]]. Link: https://www.sandromaglione.com/articles/getting-started-with-pixel-art

- **Author:** Sandro Maglione (indie developer)
- **Kind:** Primary-ish (a practitioner's notes)
- **How I read it (2026-10-01):** Read in full. Updated 4 August 2023; about six minutes long.

## What it covers

A solo game developer's notes on why pixel art suits small teams, sizes, "perfect lines", doubles and jaggies, curves, HSL colour and tiles.

## Key points, in my words

- Pixel art keeps scope manageable for one person; animations often need only 4 to 10 frames.
- Pixel counts: 16 by 16 is 256, 32 by 32 is 1,024, and 144 by 144 is 20,736, so effort and required drawing skill grow with size.
- Perfect lines have simple ratios (0:1, 1:2, 1:1, 2:1, 1:0); doubles and jaggies should be avoided unless done on purpose.
- Curves use steadily changing run lengths; isometric work uses the 2:1 line (about 26.56 degrees) because 30 degrees cannot be drawn exactly.
- Prefers HSL for picking colours and links to a hue-shifting video and to Lospec.
- Tile advice: study how existing tiles use colours and clumps; use several variants and hand-adjust repeats; manage contrast.

## Best for

The "perfect lines" list and the pixel-count arithmetic.

## Caveats

Short, and it covers the basics in a sentence each. It points to six further resources (Drububu, the Les Forges course, Slynyrd and Studio Miniboss among them).
