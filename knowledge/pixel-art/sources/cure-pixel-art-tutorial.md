Part of the [[projects/shadow-jog/knowledge/pixel-art/README|pixel-art library]]. Link: https://pixeljoint.com/forum/forum_posts.asp?TID=11299

- **Author:** Cure (posted 27 November 2010; edited by a moderator in 2014 and 2015)

## What it covers

What pixel art is and is not, where to start, terms (anti-aliasing, dithering, clusters), things to avoid, and how to build a palette with ramps and hue shifting.

## Key points, in my words

- Pixel art is defined by control, not by tools or colour count; oekaki (drawn with line and fill but without pixel awareness) is not it, though you need not place every pixel by hand.
- Start small with a limited palette; a four-colour Game Boy palette suits beginners because only value matters.
- Manual AA only; too much, too little and "AA banding" are all faults; jaggies are fixed by uniform run lengths or AA.
- Banding means pixels lining up, with named cases (hugging, fat pixels, skip-one, 45 degree); pillow shading follows flat shapes and not form (a frontal light is allowed).
- Noise is independent pixels; justified exceptions are specular highlights and tiny essential details.
- "Sel-out" is described as broken-outline AA that suits only known backgrounds.
- Small palettes give cohesion and control; saturation burns the eyes more in light than in pigment; ramps can share colours; hue-shifted ramps beat straight ones.

## Best for

The clearest account of what makes pixel art pixel art, plus the best treatment of clusters, noise and banding.

## Caveats

Its sel-out definition differs from Derek Yu's and Lospec's (Module 6). The thread leans on older tools and monitors.
