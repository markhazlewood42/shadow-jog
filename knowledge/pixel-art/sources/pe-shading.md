## What it covers

Flat fill to five-value shading on the same sphere, light direction, specular and rim light, and the common mistakes.

## Key points, in my words

- Decide the light source first and keep every zone consistent with it; rough the shading out in grayscale before adding colour.
- Tone count by size: 2 or 3 values under 24 pixels, 3 to 5 for 32 to 64, 5 to 8 for large art.
- A specular highlight belongs on the surface near the light-facing peak, inset from the outline.
- Pillow shading follows the outline instead of a light and reads as a puffy or concave shape.
- Hard transitions suggest facets and soft ones suggest curves; decompose objects into spheres, cylinders, cubes and cones.
- Dithering blends the border between value zones, while shading decides where the zones go.

## Best for

Concrete numbers for how many tones to use, and a clear statement of the specular-placement rule.

## Caveats

The numbers are guidance and not measured facts. Pair with Cure for the subtler point that pillow shading fails by ignoring form, not by facing front.
