## What it covers

A beginner guide that moves from canvas size and the grid, through lines, palettes and shading, to outlines, with a step-by-step first-sprite workflow and a FAQ.

## Key points, in my words

- Pixel art treats every pixel as a deliberate choice, and constraint (small canvases, small palettes) is presented as the art form itself.
- Gives a canvas-size ladder: 16 by 16 for tiny icons, 32 by 32 for classic characters, then 64 and up for portraits and scenes, and tells beginners to start at 16 or 32.
- Palette advice: value contrast matters more than hue, shadows shift hue, begin with an established palette (PICO-8, Endesga 32, Game Boy), and fewer colours means more cohesion.
- Six habits: start small, limit the palette to 4 to 8 colours, check at 100 percent, think in silhouettes first, commit to one light direction, study existing art pixel by pixel.
- Lists the standard beginner mistakes (similar hues, inconsistent line runs, directionless shading, too much detail, non-integer scaling, black outlines everywhere).
- The FAQ separates pixel art from low-resolution art by intention: pixel art has no blending the artist did not place.

## Best for

A tidy beginner checklist, the canvas-size table and the six-step workflow.

## Caveats

Some figures are asserted without support (the NES colour count differs from other sources, see Module 14). Use it as a summary, and lean on the primary craft sources for reasoning.
