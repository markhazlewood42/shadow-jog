## What it covers

Frames, timing, key poses, squash and stretch, anticipation, follow-through, a four-frame walk breakdown, frame rates and loop checking.

## Key points, in my words

- The stride is the key pose of a walk; mirrored, it gives a four-frame cycle.
- Hold frames (repeating a frame) are the main easing tool in a medium with no tweening; vary durations short for fast moves and long for holds.
- Squash and stretch must preserve volume; anticipation and follow-through stop actions looking mechanical.
- Use a bouncing ball to learn timing; check loops at speed and with onion skin.
- States 8 frames per second as the "industry standard" with 12 for impacts and 4 to 6 for ambient loops.

## Best for

A clean summary of the principles for a beginner.

## Caveats

The frame-rate claims have no cited support and I found no second source for them; treat them as starting points.
