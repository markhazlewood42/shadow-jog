## What it covers

Hue, saturation and value; ramps and how long they should be; hue shifting; warm light and cool shadow; a four-step recipe for a limited palette.

## Key points, in my words

- Value is the most important property; if the art reads in grayscale the values are right, and values should be fixed before hue.
- Ramp length grows with sprite size: 2 or 3 steps for tiny work, 3 for 16 pixels, 4 or 5 at 32, 5 to 7 at 64.
- Hue shifting (cool shadows, warm highlights) mimics real light, but it is a guideline: moonlight, fire, neon and underwater scenes follow other temperature logic, and what matters is that light and shadow differ in temperature.
- Palette recipe: set value steps in grays, choose one key hue, apply hue shifting, add one complementary accent.
- Colours must be judged in context, because simultaneous contrast changes how a swatch looks.
- Mistakes: all mid-values, over-saturated shadows, using every hue at equal weight.

## Best for

A compact explanation of ramps and the value-first method.

## Caveats

Same reliability note as the rest of the site. The warm and cool rule is stated more firmly in other sources than it should be, and this page is one of the places that qualifies it.
