# Shadow Jog — Glossary

> **Status: draft, waiting for Mark's review.** Mark reviews the whole glossary at once when the alpha phase is done
> (decided 2026-09-29). Until then this is the single place for the setting's names, words and ideas. Anything
> that reads like a borrowed term, or that Mark hasn't approved yet, is marked **[review]**.
>
> **Keeping it current:** when a new name, place, faction, slang word or mechanic appears in the game's text,
> add it here in the same change. When a term changes, change it here and in the code together. Where a line
> comes from the game, the source file is given so it can be checked.

The setting borrows the *feel* of the magic-meets-megacorp genre, but its names and terms are its own (the GDD's
opening rule). Section 1 lists where Shadow Jog deliberately uses its own word instead of the genre's usual one.

---

## 1. Terms chosen over the genre's usual words **[review]**

Swapped in round 11 (commit `3e24857`) after a reviewer flagged Shadowrun-specific vocabulary. All four are
**[review]**: each is a one-word change back if Mark prefers the original flavour.

| Shadow Jog says | Meaning | Instead of | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Woken** (adj.); **Woke** (verb) | Born with, or come into, magic. A Woken kid, a Woken forest; the Lurker "Woke in the flooded junction". | Awakened | Glowrat and Lurker lore, the K-M newsfeed, bar rumours, the Project Vessel log |
| **spark** | The magic in a Woken person: a measurable reserve. What Kessler-Mori extracts ("spark yield"). | essence | The cryopod label, the Project Vessel log, the K-M Focus Rod, the enemy move *Spark Drain* |
| **deck jockey**, or **jockey** | A hacker who rides a cyberdeck into systems. Hex's role. | decker | Hex's role label, the story (Pale, Dutch, Knuckles, Hex), the K-M checkpoint, item descriptions |
| **ki brawler** | A fighter whose magic comes out through the body. Kit's role. | physical adept | Kit's role label, the GDD |

**Kept, because they're older and broader than any one game:** street samurai (Gibson), deck and cyberdeck, fixer,
chrome, runner, shaman, orc, dwarf, mana, smartgun, wetware.

**Deliberately not used:** Awakened, essence, decker, physical adept, metahuman, metatype, Johnson (as a word for
a client), nuyen, SIN. (Mr. Pale is the client; the game never calls him a "Johnson".)

## 2. The world

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Saltreach** | The city: a drowned coastal megacity, 2079. Its canals were streets, and its streets were sea. |
| **2079** | The present day ("SALTREACH, 2079." opens the game). |
| **The magic came back** | Thirty years before the story (about 2049). "It didn't fix anything. The corporations just found new things to own." (intro panels) |
| **The flood of ’61** | The disaster that drowned the Lower Wards and the Sinkline metro. "The last train left in ’61." The Drowned Saint keeps a lantern for every name. |
| **The ’71 riots** | Civil unrest ten years after the flood; the Saltreach PD's street drones date from then (Street Drone lore). |
| **The Wards** | Saltreach's districts. "Three wards over" is how far the crew surface after the betrayal. |
| **The Lower Wards** | The poor, flood-damaged wards where Chapter 1 takes place. |
| **The arcology** | Kessler-Mori's tower-city north of the Lower Wards, beyond the highway. The road to it is sealed; Rook is taken up there at the end of the chapter. |
| **Neo-Lagos** | Another city, somewhere else. It's where people go when they quit Saltreach (Dutch's last jockey; Knuckles, threatened). |
| **Kowloon-era** | An old, pre-flood period, remembered in objects (the Lucky Coin; Kowloon Threads, Auntie Wen's shop). **[review]**: it borrows a real place name. |

## 3. Places

### Lantern Row (the Row): the town hub
Home turf for Rook and Kit, in the Saltreach Lower Wards. Paved over a canal basin; rain still pools where the
water used to be.

| Place | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The Drowned Saint** | Dutch's bar, on the north side of the street. A fixer's office in all but name. The house special is also called the Drowned Saint ("mostly drain cleaner"). Holds the job board, the jukebox that plays one song, and the ’61 memorial lanterns. |
| **Doc Yun's Clinic** | Treatment (revives, cures) and a pharmacy. |
| **Sleeptube 24H** | The capsule hotel (the inn). "Clean tubes, working locks, no questions." |
| **Mama Ono's** | Noodle shop. Sells Ono's Noodles (a party-wide heal). |
| **KWIK-MART 24/7** | Consumables. |
| **Last Rites Arms** | Weapons, run by Brother Tomas. "Every blade here is blessed. The guns are merely loaded." (Was Iron Saint Arms until 2026-09-29: too close to the Drowned Saint.) |
| **Kowloon Threads** | Armour and mods, run by Auntie Wen. |
| **Rook's flat, Hex's den** | On the south row. |
| **Lower Wards Access Point Seven** | A checkpoint closed by K-M Civic Security. |
| **Public terminal** | "Lantern Row. Saltreach Lower Wards. Population: unknown. Flood level: manageable. Have a K-M day." |

### The Sprawl: the world map
The Lower Wards between the neighbourhoods.

| Place | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The Barrens** | The broken eastern half: ruins, dead cars, scrap hounds. The Rustyard is out here. |
| **Hollowmere Park** | A Woken forest. A wayside shrine stands deep inside it. |
| **The canal** | Runs along the south. Nothing bites in it since ’61 (the Canal Fisher). |
| **The fallen dome** | A landmark in the west blocks. |
| **Static Mary's lot** | An empty lot off the west side street, with her radio mast over it. |
| **The K-M checkpoint** | On the road north to the arcology. "Registered guests only." |

### The Rustyard
A scavenger camp in the Barrens, squeezed by the Rustfang gang. Old Mags trades the best salvage in the Wards.

| Place | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The scrap maze** | Heaps that shift every time it rains. The scavs repaint their arrows to match. The middle board reading **SINKHOLE!** is a scav lie that keeps Rustfangs away from the kids' stash (Tobin painted it). |
| **The tire depot** | Knuckles' headquarters, at the north end. The whole Rustyard was a tire factory before the flood; before that a swamp, and before that the sea (the Old Scav). |
| **The west heap** | Where the camp leaves the Rustfangs' tribute. |

### The Sinkline: the flooded metro
Drowned since ’61. Folk who go down hear commuters.

| Place | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Platform 2**, **the concourse** | The station level. Wire lives in a corner of the concourse. |
| **Junction 4** | The flooded crossing to the east. |
| **Signal Island 4** | A catwalk ring round a sump in the junction: the Lurker's lair. Cut off until the junction is drained. |
| **The pump station, Intakes 1–3** | The drainage system. The intakes open lowest pressure first (the valve puzzle); the pump console then drains the junction. |
| **The sealed closet** | A maintenance closet walled up in ’61; its wall has cracked. |

### K-M Annex 7
A Kessler-Mori research annex under the Sinkline. Officially decommissioned. Officially.

| Place | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The lattice** | A curtain of security beams, fed by **relays** A, B and C. Cycling a relay flips every emitter it feeds, and a refit rewired B and C without updating the memo. |
| **The cryo wing** | Rows of cryopods: Project Vessel's subjects. Sable is in pod S-7. |
| **The armory** | Weapon cases (the chapter's best gear, found, never sold). |
| **K-M Requisition** | A supply terminal that still accepts a missing man's badge ("D. Petrov, Facilities"). |
| **The Warden chamber** | A plated deck inside a binding circle, cabled to pylons. |
| **The freight lift** | Up to Loading Dock 7. |
| **Loading Dock 7** | Where Mr. Pale said to bring the "data core". The betrayal happens here, in the rain. |

| Name | What it is |
|---|---|

## 5. People

### The crew

> **Pronouns (Mark, 2026-10-03):** Kit she/her, Rook he/him, **Hex they/them**, **Sable he/him**. Hex was she/her and Sable was they/them before this date; the game text, the GDD and the setting bible were changed together.

| Name | Who they are |
|---|---|
| **Kit** (she/her) | 19, human, born in Lantern Row and raised by Rook. A ki brawler: her magic came late (last month) and comes out through her fists. The protagonist. Reckless, loyal, funny. Resource: **KI**. |
| **Rook** (he/him) | 41, human street samurai. "More chrome than conscience, or so he says." Twenty years of runs. He walked past a tank with somebody still in it once, and has told himself every night since that it wasn't his job. Has no TP; fights with limited-use **skill charges**. Taken at the end of the chapter. A level-10 veteran who starts the chapter **wounded** (a stitched side from his last run, and reflex wiring that misfires); Hex re-tunes the wiring, Sable closes the wound. **[new]** |
| **Hex** (they/them) | 34, dwarf deck jockey. Genius, insomniac, anxious and sarcastic; owes money to Dutch and everyone else. Resource: **RAM**. |
| **Sable** (he/him) | 24, orc shaman, crow-sworn. Found in K-M's cryopod S-7, "Asset S-7 · VESSEL", with a spark yield of 94%. Doesn't remember how long he was in the tank. Resource: **MANA**. |
| **The crow** | Sable's totem spirit. It screamed the whole time Sable was in the tank; it follows the vans up the arcology at the end. |

| Name | Who they are |
|---|---|

| **Mr. Pale** | The client. White suit, red visor, exact hours, and a satsuma he peels in one unbroken spiral, counts, and never eats. Works for Kessler-Mori. Clerk's voice: "Contractors are paid on completion. You have completed." |
| **Old Mags** ("Auntie Mags" to the camp kids) | Runs the Rustyard scav camp and its salvage trade. Walks with a cane. Has the Stingray coprocessor Hex needs. |
| **Hedda** | Mags' sister. Her cart on Lantern Row carries Mags' stock in town once the Rustyard owes the crew (at Mags' prices, discount included). |
| **"Knuckles" Tran** | Rustfang enforcer at the tire depot; both hands are chrome. The chapter's first mini-boss. |
| **Doc Yun** | Runs the clinic. Getting back the stolen med-case is a side job. (The game never gives Doc Yun's pronouns.) |
| **Mama Ono** | Runs the noodle shop. Her lost orange cat, **Noodle** (one ear), is a side job. |
| **Auntie Wen** | Kowloon Threads. Calls Kit "Rook's girl"; sewed a ballistic lining into a customer's wedding dress. |
| **Brother Tomas** | Last Rites Arms. Blesses the blades. |
| **Saint** | The Drowned Saint's bartender: drinks and rumours. |
| **Wire** | Lives in the Sinkline concourse; sells black-market stock at **Wire's Stash**. "I'm not K-M. I live here. Rent's free." |

| **The Old Runner** | A Drowned Saint regular who remembers Rook's old crew and their move, the Thunder Rift. |

| **Dmitri Petrov** ("D. Petrov, Facilities") | An Annex worker, missing at the evacuation. His badge is still live, and his camp is behind a loose panel in the west utility corridor. He pocketed the Prototype Chip "knowing what was coming". |

## 6. Magic and the spirit world

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Woken**, **spark** | See section 1. |
| **Ki** | The body's magic: Kit's resource and style (Ki Arts). |
| **Mana** | Magic as a substance and a damage type; Sable's resource. K-M's extraction turns spark into a "transferable mana substrate". |
| **Shaman** | A spirit-worker (Sable). Heals, calls spirits, burns. |
| **Totem** | A shaman's patron spirit (Sable's crow). |
| **Spirits** | Born of places and neglect (smog wisps), of the drowned (drowned shades), or caged by K-M wards (bound spirits). Physical blades pass through them; fire and ki don't. |
| **Wards, binding circles** | K-M's corporate binding glyphs: cages for spirits, and the lock round the Warden's core. |
| **Thaumaturge** | A corporate word for a magician (the K-M Arcanist). |

## 7. Tech and chrome

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Chrome** | Cyberware; a chromed person is one with a lot of it (Rook, Knuckles' hands). |
| **Cyberdeck**, **deck** | A jockey's rig. Hex's has a whip antenna and a coprocessor slot. Stickered plating ("HEX" on tape, a cat, hazard tape, a voided warranty seal), hand-lettered violet function keys, a co-processor bay and two expansion slots. Shown close up when they talk about it, in battle when they run a program, and on the menu's Deck page. |
| **Coprocessor** | The deck's accelerator. The **Stingray** (a brand with a little fin logo) is the one Hex needs, and it "costs more than this building". |
| **RAM** | Hex's resource: working memory for programs. |
| **Programs** | Hex's techs: Spike, Scramble, Patch, Firewall, Overload, Hijack. |
| **Smartgun**, **smartpistol** | Weapons linked to targeting data (Rook's Quickdraw; Hex's Smartpistol). |
| **Trodes** | Electrodes worn on the head; faster than a jack (the Trode Net). |
| **Wetware**, **neural lace** | Brain implants (the K-M Neural Lace). |
| **Drones** | Street drones (old Saltreach PD), maintenance drones, hunter drones (K-M). |
| **The Warden** | Annex 7's security platform: a mech with a bound spirit for a core. The **Warden protocol** is the Annex's breach response. |

## 9. Game terms (names the player sees)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **TP** | The umbrella name for a member's resource: KI (Kit), RAM (Hex), MANA (Sable). Rook has none. |
| **Battle Test** | (Dev tool, not in the game.) The Battle Stage Editor's button that runs a real fight on the stage as it is in the page, saved or not. Named after RPG Maker's. A **move** there is a frame list in `src/data/moves.json`; a **still** is one picture of a move with its stand-on point (**axis**). |
| **Techs** | Abilities paid for in TP (Ki Arts, programs, spirit-work). |
| **Skills** | Abilities with a limited number of uses per rest (all of Rook's, plus a few for everyone). |
| **Combos** | Two or three members' moves fused when ordered in the same round. Thunder Rift, Target Lock, Ghost Circuit, Pyre Storm, Spirit Walk, Crow's Wing, Lifeline, Blackout, and **Clean Job** (the three-part one). Each has a caller who says a line. Since 2026-09-29 Spirit Walk needs abilities past Chapter 1's levels, so it waits for a later chapter. |
| **Timed presses** | A ring closes on the target as a blow comes in: press on the beat to **strike** harder or **brace** against a hit. A press off the beat (a **whiff**) costs. |
| **Guard** | Brace for the round. A blow taken on a guard gives TP back, and gives Rook a spent charge once a fight. |
| **Tells** | An enemy winding up something big (a turret spinning up, an arcanist surging, the Warden's cannon charging). |
| **Elements** | Phys, Fire, Shock, Mana, Cyber. Each has a **damage-type symbol** (a blade, a flame, a bolt, a chip, a four-pointed spark) shown beside every attack, tech, skill and item in the battle menus and in the weakness readouts (added 2026-09-29). |
| **Wounded** | Rook's condition for most of Chapter 1: less HP, ATK and AGI, a charge short on every skill, and some skills locked (listed greyed on his Status page). Hex's re-tune halves it and gives two skills back; Sable closes it. |
| **Level-up** | Rarer since 2026-09-29 (Chapter 1 ends around level 6; the full game near 30). Each one is a full recovery (HP, TP, charges), and new abilities come from a level only three times in Chapter 1; the rest come from the story. |
| **Deck** (menu page) | Hex's cyberdeck, close up: the Stingray in its co-processor slot, two empty **expansion slots** (where later chapters' parts go), and their programs. |
| **Two of a kind** | Enemies of the same kind in one fight are lettered (Glowrat A, Glowrat B), in their names, the turn strip and, on machines, a stencil. |
| **Families** | Human, Machine, Beast, Spirit, Ghoul (each with its own weaknesses). |
| **Bestiary**, **Places**, **Combo log** | The menu's records. |
| **NOW chip** | On the side-view battle's turn timeline, the portrait at the left that shows whose turn it is; the next turns follow along the line (heroes above it, enemies below, with a red rim and a pink corner flag). The one acting **next** has an amber glow; after a round tick the line previews the following round in dim chips. |
| **Hero colour** | Each hero's one colour (Kit coral, Rook sand, Hex lilac, Sable cream; `color` in `src/data/party.ts`). The side-view HUD uses it for the name in the party table, the rim of the hero's turn chip and the name tab over the acting hero. Cyan is not a hero colour: it always means "the one acting now" (the ring, the table row, the tab's stripe). |
| **Bottom band** | In the side-view battle, the party table, the command strip and the enemy box sit along the bottom as one window with a divider between them. During an action the command strip stays in its slot, dimmed. |
| **Command codes** | The three-letter names under the command icons (ATK, SKL, CMB, ITM, GRD) so no icon is a mystery; the lit icon also gets its full name and cost (or a one-line hint) on the strip's caption line. |
| **Hit counter** | The small box at the top right while an action plays: how many hits the crew has landed this round, and their total damage. |
| **Stage** (battleground) | (Dev tool word.) One place where fights happen, such as the street or the sewer: backdrop, horizon and floor, depth rows, and where the heroes and each enemy count stand. Data in `src/data/stages.json` (the 480x270 layout) and `src/data/stages-640.json` (the 640x360 layout the shipped battle uses under `?engine=sje`, made from the first by `scripts/stage-640.mjs`); drawn by `BattleStageScene` (`src/battlestage/`, M3). A map names it per area with `bg`. It does not say WHO fights (that is the encounter, an RPG Maker "troop"). |
| **Mirror** (enemy facing) | (Dev tool word.) Flipping an enemy's picture left-to-right so it looks at the heroes, who stand on the left. Which enemy sprites are mirrored is `src/data/enemyfacing.json`; the Battle Stage Editor's "Mirror (face the heroes)" switch edits it for every appearance of that enemy. |
| **Hero proportions** (Height, Build) | (Dev tool word.) How tall and how broad each hero stands in battle, as a multiple of the drawn picture: Kit and Rook are human, Hex is a dwarf (shorter, stouter), Sable is an orc (taller, broad). Two numbers per hero in `src/data/heroes.json`, the same in every battle; the Battle Stage Editor's "Proportions" group edits them. Made by adding or removing whole rows and columns of pixels, never by stretching (`docs/CONCEPTS.md`). |
| **HUD frame** | (Dev word.) The one rectangle that every battle HUD piece is placed from: the menus, the status cards, the turn strip, the top text band, the target box, the cut-ins and the banners. It is the whole screen, so the HUD hugs the screen edges (Mark's choice at Review 2, D6 of `docs/PIVOT-640.md`). `HUD_FRAME` and `hudLayout` in `src/scenes/battlekit/geom.ts`. |
| **Surround** | (Dev word.) What fills the screen around a map that is smaller than the view (decision D7): **b1** an edge fill for an indoor map, **b2** a themed surround (a yard fence, a dock quay over water) for an outdoor map. Mark's rule, Review 3. `src/scenes/fieldkit/surround.ts`, one table keyed by map id. |
| **Dialog cap** | (Dev word.) The dialog box is at most 464 px wide and centered (decision D8), so its text is 448 px wide (392 with a portrait): the width the box had at 480x270. `DIALOG_MAX_W` in `src/ui/layout.ts`. |
| **Pane cap** | (Dev word.) The field menu's list panes (Items, Techs, the equip slots, Save, the objective box) are at most 364 px wide (decision D8); Items and Techs keep the party in compact cards in the strip beside them. `MENU_PANE_MAX_W`, `menuPaneW()` in `src/ui/layout.ts`. |
| **Layout recorder** | (Dev word.) A fake canvas for tests that writes down every rectangle, image and clip a scene draws, and the box of each text and window, so a test can check the layout in node. `tests/recorder.ts`; used by `tests/ui-layout.test.ts`. |
| **Title layout** | (Dev word.) The title screen's composition as plain data: the base lines of the three city layers, the spire, the moon, the roof, the monorail, the rain count and the logo's place. Right and bottom pieces hang from the world's edges. `src/scenes/title-layout.ts`. |
| **Page offset** | (Dev word.) `PAGE_DY` (`src/ui/layout.ts`): half the extra height, 45 px at 640x360. It moves the results page, the next-chapter card and Game over, which were composed top-down for 270 rows, down to the middle of the screen. |
| **Panel frame** | (Dev word.) The area the comic panels may use: 8 px in from the left, top and right edges and clear of the footer, `PANEL_FRAME` in `src/scenes/panels.ts` (624 by 334 at 640x360). The 17 panel rects are authored for it, and a test checks that each lies inside. |
| **Pop-in item** | (Dev word.) One entry of the D17 list: content that the 640x360 view shows early, cropped or too soon (P1 to P4). Each had option **a** (limit the camera) and **b** (a curtain or a hold); the table ships the one Mark picked. `src/scenes/fieldkit/popins.ts`; the list is in the Record of `docs/PIVOT-640.md`. |
| **Curtain** | (Dev word.) A feathered dark box over part of the map that hides what the wider view would show too early, until the player is near or while one event runs. Shipped for P1 and P2. |
| **Global HUD** | The one battle HUD layout every fight uses. In `src/data/hud.json` (480x270) and `src/data/hud-640.json` (640x360), drawn by `Hud` in `src/battlestage/hud.ts` (M3). Without the flag the old battle HUD is still placed in code from `HUD_FRAME` in `src/scenes/battlekit/geom.ts`. A stage may override single boxes; it then keeps only the fields that differ. The Battle Stage Editor calls this switch "Different on this stage". |
| **Depth haze** | In the side-view battle, fighters on the back rows are blended a little toward the stage's fog colour, so distance reads without shrinking anyone. |
