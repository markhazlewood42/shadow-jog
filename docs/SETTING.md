# Saltreach, 2079 — Setting Bible

> **What this is:** the world behind Shadow Jog: its history, politics, society, key events and people. It's written
> as a working document for Mark to reshape to his own vision in future updates.
>
> **How to read it:** everything is tagged.
> - **[canon]**: already said or shown in the game (Chapter 1). Changing it means changing game text too.
> - **[new]**: invented for this document on 2026-09-29 to fill a gap. Not in the game yet, so change it freely.

## 1. The shape of it

Saltreach is a drowned coastal megacity [canon]. Its canals were streets, and its streets were sea. Thirty years ago
the magic came back, and "it didn't fix anything. The corporations just found new things to own." [canon] In 2079
the city is two cities stacked on top of each other [new]:

- **Up top:** the reclaimed wards, the corporate enclaves and the arcology, dry behind sea walls the corporations own.
- **Down below:** the Lower Wards, which the flood of ’61 took and nobody took back. People live there anyway,
  and make their own rules.

The story's engine is the gap between the two. Kessler-Mori has learned that magic in people (**spark**) can be
measured, extracted and sold. The Lower Wards are full of people who have it and nobody to protect them.

**Tone:** neon noir in the rain. Working people, dry humour, small kindnesses, and a corporation that talks like an
HR memo while it does monstrous things [canon, the game's voice].

## 3. The city and its wards

### Saltreach
A port city on a cold northern coast, built on reclaimed marsh [new]. Population uncounted since ’61 [canon, the
public terminal: "Population: unknown."].

### The Upper Wards [new]
The reclaimed city: dry streets, working transit, clean water and private police. Ward names follow whoever rebuilt
them: **Halden Quay**, **Mori Heights**, **Vey Crescent**. Lower Warders call them simply "up top".

### The Arcology ("the Spire") [canon: the arcology; name new]
Kessler-Mori's tower-city north of the Lower Wards, beyond the highway. It holds housing, offices, labs and a
private hospital, and it's where Rook is taken at the end of Chapter 1 [canon]. The road to it is sealed at a K-M
checkpoint for "registered guests only" [canon].

### Harbor Ward [canon: name]
The docks. Halden Tidewater's territory [new]: container stacks, the wall's control stations, bonded warehouses.
It breeds runners (the Glass Wolves were Harbor Ward [canon]) and smugglers who run the **Lagos Line**, the ships
to Neo-Lagos [new].

### The Lower Wards [canon]
Unreclaimed since ’61 [new: the legal status]. Everything in Chapter 1 happens here.

- **Lantern Row** [canon]: the street that stayed dry, and home to Rook and Kit. The Drowned Saint, Doc Yun's, Mama
  Ono's, Sleeptube 24H, Kwik-Mart, Last Rites Arms and Kowloon Threads.
- **The Sprawl** [canon]: the Lower Wards between neighbourhoods.
  - **The Barrens** [canon]: the broken east, ruins and wrecks.
  - **Hollowmere Park** [canon]: the old city park, which Woke during the Blue Hour [new] and is now a forest that
    moves when nobody's watching [new]. Its wayside shrine [canon] is tended by canal shamans [new].
  - **The fallen dome** [canon]: the Saltreach Planetarium, which collapsed in ’61 [new].
- **The Rustyard** [canon]: the old **Brannock Tire Works** [new], now a scav camp under Old Mags.
- **The Sinkline** [canon]: the flooded metro. Line 4 [new] still has its train in the tunnels.
- **Annex 7** [canon]: K-M's lab under the Sinkline.

### Elsewhere
- **Neo-Lagos** [canon]: the city people leave Saltreach for [canon]. A rising West African megacity with open
  borders for the Recast and no registration laws [new]. Half a promise, half a rumour.
- **The Kowloon Quarter** [new]: a Saltreach district founded by Hong Kong emigrants in the 2030s and drowned in ’61.
  "Kowloon-era" [canon] means pre-flood Saltreach. (The glossary flags "Kowloon" for review, and this is one
  in-world reason it could stay.)

## 4. Political state (2079) [new unless tagged]

### Who actually rules
| Power | Holds | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Kessler-Mori (K-M)** [canon] | The arcology, Mori Heights, the Lower Wards policing contract, the Registration office | Money, walls, Civic Security, and the Assembly's debts |
| **Halden Tidewater** | The sea walls, the harbour, Harbor Ward | Whoever controls the walls controls the tide. Halden reminds the city of this every storm season. |
| **Vey Consolidated** | The newsfeeds, public terminals, the transit authority | It owns what the city hears. The K-M Newsfeed [canon] runs on Vey infrastructure. |
| **The Saltreach Assembly** | Nothing much | An elected council that meets, votes, and signs what the Compact tells it to. |
| **Mayor Joaquim Tell** | A title | Third term, genial, powerless, and fond of ribbon-cutting ceremonies up top. |
| **The gangs** | The unreclaimed Lower Wards, block by block | Tribute, fear, and being there when Civic Security isn't. The **Rustfangs** [canon] hold the Barrens. |
| **The fixers** | Nothing, officially | Every job in the Wards goes through someone like Dutch [canon]. |

### The Reclamation Compact (2062)
The founding deal of modern Saltreach. The corporations rebuilt the walls and took the dry wards as payment. The
Lower Wards were meant to be "reclaimed in phases". Phase two has been "under review" for seventeen years. Because
they're unreclaimed, the Lower Wards have no city services, no voting ward, and no court: they have a policing
contract, which is K-M's.

### The Woken Registration Act (2072)
Every Woken person has to register their abilities with the Registration office, which K-M administers under
contract ("free, safe, and mandatory" [canon]). A registration record includes a **spark assessment**, a number
people aren't told they have been given. The unregistered risk fines, then detention. In the Lower Wards most people
don't register, and Civic Security mostly doesn't check, until someone upstairs wants a number.

### Tensions going into Chapter 2
- **The K-M–Halden merger talks.** K-M wants the walls; Halden wants spark power for its pumps. A merger needs a
  clean audit, which is why Vessel's leftover assets suddenly matter (see the timeline, 2079).
- **Phase two.** A rumour says the merger will finally "reclaim" the Lower Wards: by pumping them dry and clearing
  whoever lives there.
- **Radio Static** [canon] is the only voice nobody paid for, and K-M would very much like to know where Static
  Mary broadcasts from.

## 5. Social state [new unless tagged]

### Life in the Lower Wards
- **Water** decides everything. Streets flood on the spring tides, and the canal is poison [canon: "Nothing bites
  in the canal"]. Filtered water is money: the Rustyard's stolen water filter [canon] mattered.
- **Housing:** capsules (Sleeptube [canon]), squatted upper floors, houseboats on the canals, and whatever the
  heaps allow.
- **Work:** salvage, repair, noodle stands, clinics, bars, and **runs**. Runners [canon] take the jobs nobody
  signs for. A **jog** [canon] is the small, safe kind; there are fewer of those every year.
- **Money:** **cred** (¢) [canon], on sticks and chips. There are no banks down here, only fixers who hold
  money for a cut.
- **Order:** gangs collect **tribute** [canon]. Civic Security raids, then leaves. Neighbours sort out the rest.

### The Woken
People who have magic [canon]. About one in thirty Lower Warders shows some sign of it, and most hide it. The
Blue Hour children (born 2039–2049) are the most likely to be Woken. Kit's late blooming, at 19 [canon: "Started
last month"], is rare, and rare is what K-M looks for.

### The Recast ("folk")
Dwarves, orcs and the long-eared [canon: all three appear]. Up top, the Recast are tolerated as a workforce. Down
below, nobody much cares. Orcs are still stopped at checkpoints more often than anyone else; dwarves are thought of
as good with machines, which Hex finds tiresome and accurate.

### Faith and memory
- **The lantern custom:** one lantern per name lost to the water, hung on the anniversary of the flood (14 October).
  The Drowned Saint keeps its memorial wall lit all year [canon: "a lantern for every name. There is room for
  more."].
- **The Drowned Saint** itself: a statue of Saint Brendan, patron of sailors, found standing upright in the flooded
  chapel after ’61. Dutch built the bar around it. The house drink is named for him [canon: "mostly drain
  cleaner"].
- **The Order of Last Rites:** a militant lay order, of which Brother Tomas [canon] is the last in the Wards.
  Blessed blades and merely loaded guns [canon].
- **Canal shamanism:** a spirit practice that grew up on the houseboats after the Return. Totem spirits (Sable's
  crow [canon]), offerings at the Hollowmere shrine, and a strong taboo against binding spirits, which is what K-M
  does for a living.

### Street culture
- Runners are **crews** [canon]. Crews have names (the Glass Wolves [canon]) and reputations, and they remember
  their dead.
- Code: "Two rules, kid. Get paid. And don't die for anyone who isn't paying." [canon, Rook]
- Music: one jukebox song nobody dares unplug [canon]; Radio Static [canon]; canal-boat work songs.
- Food: noodles (Mama Ono's [canon]), canal-grown greens, vat protein, and eel, never from the canal.

## 6. Magic, spirits and spark [new unless tagged]

- **What came back in 2049:** a second current alongside electricity. People can draw on it (the Woken), places
  soak it up (Hollowmere Park), and it gathers into **spirits** where feeling runs thick: grief (the drowned shades
  [canon]), neglect (smog wisps [canon]), or hunger (the Lurker [canon]).
- **Spark** [canon]: the reserve of magic in a Woken body. Everyone Woken has a number, and K-M's instruments read
  it as a **yield** percentage [canon: "SPARK YIELD 94%"]. A high yield is rare. Sable's 94% is extraordinary.
- **Ways of using it:**
  - **Ki** [canon]: magic through the body (Kit). Fast, instinctive, hard to measure, and so hard for K-M to
    extract; that's why Pale is interested.
  - **Shamanism** [canon]: magic through spirits (Sable). A negotiation, never a command.
  - **Thaumaturgy** [canon: the K-M Arcanist]: corporate magic. Drawn from the building's current, not the self.
    Learned at the arcology's academy.
- **Extraction** [canon: Project Vessel's log]: spark drawn from a Woken body is stabilised as a **mana substrate**
  ("power, weapons, obedience" [canon]). Power runs things. Weapons are thaumic munitions. *Obedience* is the one
  nobody says out loud: substrate fed to a **bound spirit** makes it controllable. That's how the Warden worked
  [canon: a bound spirit for a core], and why it screamed [canon].
- **Binding** [canon: wards, binding circles]: caging a spirit with corporate glyphs. Canal shamans consider it the
  worst thing a person can do.
- **Cost:** extraction doesn't kill quickly. Subjects lose memory, then time, then themselves. Sable "doesn't
  remember how long they were in the tank" [canon]; S-5 and S-6's pods "went dark" [canon].

## 7. Technology [new unless tagged]

- **Chrome** [canon]: cyberware. Cheap chrome is common down below: salvaged arms (Knuckles' hands [canon]), eyes
  and plating. Good chrome is K-M or Halden surplus.
- **The grid** [canon: Hex's Overload "dumps the grid"]: the city's networks and power, tangled together.
  Unreclaimed wards run on leeched power and pirate mesh nets.
- **Decks and deck jockeys** [canon]: rigs for riding into systems. Coprocessors decide how fast a jockey thinks
  inside one. The **Stingray** [canon] is the best there is, made by **Fathom Systems**, whose fin logo [canon] is
  famous.
- **Drones:** the abandoned Saltreach PD street drones [canon], K-M hunter drones [canon], maintenance drones still
  fixing tunnels nobody uses [canon].
- **Thaumic tech:** K-M's substrate-powered systems, including the lattice [canon], the Warden [canon] and the
  arcology's own power core.
- **Transport:** canal taxis, autocabs (the Cab Voucher [canon]), a surface tram up top, and nothing at all in the
  Lower Wards since the Sinkline drowned.

## 8. Factions

### Kessler-Mori [canon: existence, security, Newsfeed, registration, scholarships, Vessel, Annex 7]
[new:] Founded in 2034 as Kessler Pharmaceuticals and Mori Energy, merged in 2047. The first corporation to
industrialise magic. Its public face is civic responsibility: "Have a K-M day" [canon]. Its private one is
Project Vessel. Its divisions:
- **Civic Security** [canon]: police for hire. Sentinels [canon] and hunter drones [canon].
- **Registration** [canon]: the Woken census.
- **Thaumic Energy Division** [new]: Vessel's parent.
- **Asset Recovery** [new]: getting back what K-M has lost, deniably. Mr. Pale is its most senior field officer.
- **The Futures Scholarship** [canon: "scholarships"].

### Halden Tidewater [new]
Owns the walls and the harbour. Old money, maritime, pragmatic. Its failure in ’61 (the Halden Wall breach) is
never mentioned in its presence. It's courting K-M because substrate power could run its pumps for a fraction of
the cost.

### Vey Consolidated [new]
Newsfeeds, public terminals, the transit authority. It rents K-M its voice. Vey's founder, **Octavia Vey**, is 91
and has survived three corporate coups.

### Orison Biotech (defunct) [new]
The first spark lab, bought by K-M in 2064. Rook's "different logo" [canon] was Orison's.

### The Rustfangs [canon]
A Barrens gang: leather, spikes, colours that run in the rain [canon]. They tax the Rustyard [canon].
[new:] Founded by dock workers laid off when Halden automated the harbour in 2068. Knuckles [canon] was their
enforcer until the crew ran him off to Neo-Lagos [canon].

### The scavs of the Rustyard [canon]
Old Mags' people: families who salvage the Barrens. They run the painted arrows through the maze [canon]. [new:]
Most of their children are Woken, and none are registered. That's the camp's real secret, and part of why Mags
never pays the Rustfangs more than she has to.

### Runner crews [canon: runners, the Glass Wolves]
[new:] Maybe a dozen serious crews work the Lower Wards and Harbor Ward. The **Glass Wolves** [canon] (Harbor Ward,
"good ones") were led by **Ines Calder**; four went down the Sinkline and none came back. Rook's old crew was
**Low Tide**: Rook, Kit's mother, a jockey called **Juniper**, and a driver called **Sal**. They broke up in 2064.

### Radio Static [canon]
Static Mary's pirate station [canon]. [new:] It moves every week; the mast on her lot [canon] is a decoy.

## 10. Where the story stands after Chapter One

**[canon]:** Rook is taken up the arcology, hurt but alive. Kit, Hex and Sable surfaced three wards over. Pale has
ordered them found and Rook kept breathing: "I want to know who taught Miss Kit to fight like that. You have until
morning." The crew is going to get Rook, and on the way they'll ask Dutch what he knew. Hex copied Vessel's pod
logs: intake dates, yields, transfer orders, every name [canon].

**Seeds for Chapter Two, "Deniable Assets"** [title canon; everything below new]:
1. **Dutch.** He sold them to Pale, and to the Glass Wolves before them, but only because Pale holds the Drowned
   Saint's debt. His confession gives the crew a way into the arcology: Pale's own retrieval routes.
2. **The pod logs.** Every name K-M took is a lantern on the Drowned Saint's wall. Publishing the logs (through
   Radio Static?) would break the merger, and put the crew at the top of K-M's list.
3. **Mara Oduya.** Pale wants Kit because he wants Mara's line. Maybe Mara went into a tank too. Maybe she's still in
   one.
4. **Dmitri Petrov** knows the arcology's service tunnels and what was moved out of Annex 7 before the evacuation.
5. **The merger** and "phase two": a reason for the Lower Wards themselves to take a side.

## 11. Style notes for writing in this world [new]

- **Names:** mixed, like a real port city: Dutch, Nigerian, Chinese, Vietnamese, Scots, Polish, Japanese.
  Corporations get double-barrelled founder names; streets get working names (Lantern Row, the Barrens).
- **Corporate voice:** calm, passive, accounting language ("pending resolution", "line item", "contractors are paid
  on completion" [canon]). They never say "kill".
- **Street voice:** short, dry, generous underneath. Nobody explains the world to someone who lives in it.
- **Magic:** felt before it's named. Warm, strange and a little frightening, never a spell list.
- **Avoid:** the words in the glossary's "deliberately not used" list; a chosen-one prophecy; corporations that are
  evil for their own sake. K-M's horror is that it's ordinary business.
