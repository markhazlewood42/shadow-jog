# Spike: Phaser tooling spike (the side-view battle stage and its editor)

Branch `spike/phaser-stage` (draft PR, never merged), checked out next to the main project at `projects/shadow-jog-phaser`. Plan and background: `docs/PHASE-0.2.md` (decision 6 and the "How the three fit together" update), on the release-prep branch until it merges. Builds on `spike/side-battle` (the side-view spike) and its stage design (`docs/spikes/side-battle-stage.md` and `docs/spikes/stage-configs/`, coming from that branch).

## Question
If the new side-view battle stage is built natively in Phaser 4 with an in-game edit mode, can Mark design battle stages himself (horizon and floor, hero and enemy positions on depth rows, HUD layout, then a battle test), and is Phaser a better base for a "Shadow Jog Engine" toolset than the current engine?

## Why now
Mark asked (2026-10-02) for tools to make battle-design calls himself, like RPG Maker's troop placement view and battle test, instead of agents iterating on layouts by hand, and whether Phaser makes a bespoke toolset easier. Agent judging plateaued at about 7/10 on exactly these taste calls. Phaser keeps a list of on-screen objects with positions and depth and makes them draggable with a setting, so an edit mode can live inside the real battle scene; on the current immediate-mode engine every tool has to build its own hit-testing and preview. Building the editor on the current engine first would mean building its object layer twice if we port. Getting it wrong costs either a port that doesn't pay off, or tools built on a base we later leave.

## Time box
About 4-6 working sessions of Claude time. The box closes **2026-10-09** whatever state the spike is in. Step 1 is built to fail fast.

## Exit criteria (written before any code, dated 2026-10-02)
- GO if all of these hold:
  - **Mark can design a stage himself:** open the editor, move the horizon and floor, drag the four heroes and the enemies onto depth rows (overlap order and floor shadows follow automatically), move or switch the HUD layout, save, and press battle test to play a real fight on that stage, without agent help. Save-to-battle-test takes under a minute.
  - **What you drag is what the game draws:** the edit mode runs inside the same scene the battle uses, not a separate preview.
  - **The battle is real:** the fight is driven by the existing battle engine (`src/battle`, unchanged), and Rook's strike plays from Mark's Sprite Fusion frames with the lunge, hitstop and hit effect.
  - **It runs well:** 60 fps with the crew, four enemies and effects; frame time p95 under 6 ms on this machine; the spike's tests run in CI (including whatever GPU-less runners do).
  - **It is a base worth building on:** the judges (engineering and design lenses) and Mark would rather build the next tools (animation composer, troop editor, maps via Tiled) on this than on the current engine, with the reasons written down.
- NO-GO if any of these happens:
  - Getting Phaser to show the crew on a stage from a stage config takes more than 2 sessions (step 1).
  - Edit mode can't be what-you-see-is-what-you-get without a separate preview path.
  - Phaser 4 gaps (bugs, missing docs, v3-only answers) cost more than a session in total.
  - It can't run in CI.
- Informational, not gates: bundle size (Phaser is about 350 kB gzipped; Mark's dependency policy allows it), how Phaser's filters compare with our bloom, how much of the current renderer could be reused.

## Steps
1. **Shell and stage (fail-fast):** a separate DEV-only entry (for example `/stagelab.html`) running Phaser 4 at 480x270 with integer scaling and pixel-art settings; load a stage config (the side-view spike's StageConfig format) and draw the backdrop with its 3/4 floor, depth rows, contact shadows and depth sorting; Mark's four heroes (Sprite Fusion idle loops) on the left facing right, enemies on the right.
2. **Edit mode:** toggle edit; drag heroes and enemies (snap to depth rows, live overlap order and shadows), drag horizon and floor handles, move HUD regions or pick a HUD preset, a small settings panel (Tweakpane, MIT) for numbers; save to `src/data/stages.json` through a Vite dev plugin, the same way the FX lab saves `fx.json`; load the saved stage back.
3. **HUD:** the side-view HUD from the stage design (turn order, commands, party status) as Phaser objects whose regions come from the config.
4. **Battle test:** run a real fight on the stage through `src/battle`, with Rook's strike from Mark's frames (lunge, smear, hitstop, hit effect) and a basic hurt reaction.
5. **Measure and compare:** frame time, bundle size, CI; lines of code for shell, stage, editor; a written comparison with what the same editor would take on the current engine.
6. **Result:** fill in below; Mark decides GO / NO-GO.

## Step 1 notes (2026-10-02, round 1)
What was built: the stage lab at `/stagelab.html` (DEV only; listed in the DEV tab as "Stage lab (Phaser spike)"), Phaser 4.2.1, 480x270, pixel-art settings, Scale Manager in NONE mode with a whole-number zoom that re-fits on resize (2x at 960x540, 3x at 1500x900, 4x at 1920x1080).
Files: `src/stage/config.ts` (types, validator, helpers, no Phaser), `src/data/stages.json` (stage "street": horizon 124, floor 150-266, four depth rows with optional per-row tint, four party slots, enemy slot sets for 1-4, shadow), `src/stage/textures.ts` (asset pipeline), `src/stage/feet.ts` (foot anchors from pixels), `src/stage/idle.ts` (the game's enemy idle sway as a pure function), `src/stage/stagescene.ts` (the scene), `src/stage/lab.ts` (entry and the `window.__stagelab` test hook), `src/stage/metrics.ts` (frame timing), `stagelab.html`.
How it draws: the street backdrop and its neon glow from `battleBg('street')` are composed at 2x with nearest-neighbour onto one 480x270 canvas texture, the foreground rails are a second texture drawn over everything; the enemies come from `enemyArt` (the punk twice as two different individuals, and a glowrat) washed with the backdrop's ambient tint and given the glow layer; Mark's four idle sheets load through Phaser's loader using each sheet's `metadata.json` and loop at their own fps (8).
Pixels: the crew draw 1:1; the human enemies draw at 2 screen pixels per art pixel (that is how the game itself draws them: painted at its 240x135 world scale and shown at 2x) and the creatures at 1:1. So the brief's "canvas pixels are screen pixels" holds for the glowrat but not the punks, and the punks look chunkier than the crew. This is the game's own mixed grain (see CONCEPTS, "Shrinking pixel art"), not something the pipeline adds; deciding what to do about it is a design call for a later step.
Feet: Rook's cell is 79x68 and the others 64x64, and every figure fills its cell differently, so each sprite gets its origin from the pixels (`footAnchor`: the heavy group among the lowest rows' columns, averaged over the loop, and the lowest sole row). A slot's x,y is then where each one stands, for all four.
Depth: `depthFor(y, x)` (nearer draws on top, ties to the right) is the sprite's depth; its contact shadow (a pixel-art ellipse with a stippled rim) is `depth - 0.5`. Row tints are Phaser multiply tints.
The fixed step: `update` feeds an accumulator and runs `tick()` once per 1/60 s (at most 5 catch-up ticks, then the backlog is dropped). Today `tick` only moves the enemies' idle sway; the battle replay will run there.
Ready for edit mode: each fighter is a `Fighter` record (sprite, shadow, slot); `place(fighter, slot)` re-places one and `applyStage(config)` re-lays everything out from a changed config; sprites carry a `fighterId` data tag for pointer picking.
Stand-ins: Mark's sheets are git-ignored, so on a machine without them (CI) the lab draws code-made block figures through the same pipeline and says so on the page and in `__stagelab.standIns`; `?standins` forces that for testing.
Tests: `tests/stageconfig.test.ts` (loader and validator, the shipped file, draw order, tints) and `tests/stagefeet.test.ts` (foot anchors on made-up frames and on Mark's real sheets where present, idle sway, percentile and frame stats); `e2e/stagelab.spec.ts` (real assets, no console errors, non-blank, whole-number zoom and resize, every texture NEAREST, every 4x4 block of a zoom-4 screenshot one flat colour, whole-pixel positions, two scene restarts without stale fighters or extra textures, the stand-ins, the forced canvas renderer, timing) and `e2e/stagelab-nogl.spec.ts` (a browser with WebGL switched off falls back to Phaser's canvas renderer and still draws). Screenshots go to the OS temp folder (`STAGELAB_SHOTS`), never the repo; `STAGELAB_MEDIA=<folder>` writes the judges' pictures.
CI: the bundled Chromium revision this Playwright wants (1243) is not installed on this machine and installing it is a download, so `CI=1` runs were not done here. Checked instead on Edge: normal GPU, `--disable-gpu` (software WebGL, which is what a GPU-less runner gives: Phaser AUTO picks WEBGL and everything passes) and `--disable-3d-apis` (no WebGL at all: AUTO falls back to CANVAS and the stage still draws and passes). The e2e does not skip without Mark's folder, it uses the stand-ins.
Numbers (this machine, Edge, dev server, warm unless noted): first frame 360-440 ms after navigation (593 ms right after clearing Vite's dependency cache); 286-301 frames in 5 s; frame interval p50 16.7-17.5 ms and p95 16.7-18.2 ms; CPU work per frame (update plus draw calls) p50 0.1 ms and p95 0.2-0.3 ms, with or without the GPU flag (software GL work runs on another thread, so this number does not show it). Against the 6 ms p95 criterion that passes by a wide margin, but it is a quiet scene with no effects or HUD yet.
Size: a separate test build of the lab (`node scripts/stagelab-size.mjs`, scratch folder) is 1,474 kB raw and 390 kB gzip in all: Phaser 1,374 kB raw and 356 kB gzip (about the 350 kB expected), the lab's own code plus the art generators it reuses 99 kB raw and 34 kB gzip. The shipped build is unchanged: building the base commit and this one gives the same five chunk file names (content hashes) and sizes, and none contains Phaser. The bundle budget already reads "exceeded" (239.8 kB of 236 kB gzip) on the base commit, so that is the side-battle spike's growth and not this step's.
Phaser 4 surprises: (1) with `pixelArt: true` every texture's `scaleMode` still reads the default (0, "LINEAR") because the renderer decides on `antialias: false` instead, so reading the property proves nothing; every texture here is set to NEAREST explicitly and the screenshot test is the real proof. (2) The migration guide says `roundPixels` defaults to false and only rounds unscaled, axis-aligned objects, while the config docs say `pixelArt` turns it on; the 2x-scaled punks still land on whole pixels because their positions are whole numbers. (3) `MAX_ZOOM` is worked out once at start, so the zoom has to be re-fitted on the Scale Manager's `resize` event (only when it changes, or `setZoom` fires the event again forever). (4) A restarted scene is the same object, so anything it remembers (our fighters list) must be reset in `init`; the loader's listeners are cleared on shutdown but the texture list and the animation list are game-wide, so each texture, sheet and animation is guarded by an exists check. (5) `Create.GenerateTexture` and `textures.generate` are gone in v4, so the stand-in sheets are drawn on a canvas and added with `addSpriteSheet`. (6) `import Phaser from 'phaser'` type-checks under TypeScript 7 as a default import of the `export =` module. Nothing needed more than a read of the shipped `skills/` guides; no v3-only answer cost any time.

## Step 1 notes, round 2 (2026-10-02)
Fixes to the round-1 judges' findings, in their order; the round-1 notes above stay as written except where a line below corrects them.
CORRECTION to round 1 (pixel grain): the round-1 notes said the human enemies draw at 2 screen pixels per art pixel and look chunkier than the crew. That was wrong. Every enemy in the game today is painted at `res` 2 (screen resolution), so `displayScale` was already 1 for all of them, and the round-1 picture shows 1:1 grain. Measured on the textures: the share of aligned 2x2 blocks that are one flat colour is 18.5% for the punk and 11.6% for the rat, against 16.5% for Kit, 12.6% for Hex and 17.7% for Rook (a genuinely 2x-chunky sprite would be near 100%). So there is no grain mismatch to fix; the visible difference is SIZE (the punk is 97 px tall, the heroes about 55-60 px), which is a design call about enemy scale, not a pixel-grain problem. The rule is now data: `enemyScale` in `stages.json` (whole numbers, per enemy sprite key or `"*"`; shipped as `{"*": 1}` = every enemy at 1 screen pixel per art pixel) and `shadow.enemyScale` (a multiplier on each enemy's own shadow width), both applied in `StageScene.place`, so an editor can tune either. Fractional sizes are not offered (they would give uneven pixels).
Horizon and floor now do something. The baked backdrop is cut into named frames (`wall`, `floor`, plus one-pixel `wallTop` and `floorBottom` strips). The wall hangs from `stage.horizon`, the floor starts on it, and the strips stretch to fill whatever gap a move opens, so a dragged horizon always leaves a full backdrop (`layoutBackdrop`). `floor.top` and `floor.bottom` clamp where feet can land (`slotPoint`, so a row outside the band is pulled onto it) and an edit-mode guide draws the horizon, band and rows as thin lines. Picture: `media/spike-phaser/p1-r2-edit-guides-2x.png` (horizon dragged to 104, guides on).
`applyStage` now rebuilds what a change needs: a new `backdrop` rebuilds the pictures and re-washes the enemies with its light, a changed `lineup` rebuilds the party, `setEnemies(keys)` swaps the enemy set (the slot set for the new head-count is used), everything else just moves. `currentStage()` returns the config with the fighters' current slots written back (what a Save would store). Tested for leaks (display-list count and texture count do not move across rebuilds; returning to a backdrop finds its textures).
Who stands where is data: `lineup` (four crew ids) and `fight` (the demo enemies) are in `stages.json`, validated against the real crew ids and `ENEMIES` keys. `src/stage/crew.ts` (pure) maps crew ids to sheet folders; `textures.ts` only makes textures. The demo fight constant is gone from the lab.
Restarts do no repeat work: foot anchors are stored on each sheet's texture (`customData`), the backdrop generator runs only when its texture is missing (its tint is stored on the texture), and enemy art info is stored the same way.
Device-pixel zoom: the zoom is now a whole number of DEVICE pixels per game pixel, not of CSS pixels (`src/stage/zoom.ts`, `keepZoomWhole` in `boot.ts`), re-fitted on resize and when the pixel ratio changes, and the canvas is centred by hand with margins that are whole device pixels (and exact in the browser's 1/64 CSS-pixel layout unit; Playwright's Chromium blended an outer row of game pixels when a margin was not). `e2e/stagelab-dpr.spec.ts` screenshots at 125% (two window sizes, one odd), 150%, 200% and 250% and checks every game pixel is an exact k x k block of one colour. Known limit, measured: at 175% the canvas size (k * 480 / 1.75 CSS px) cannot be stored exactly by the browser and the outer row and column of game pixels can be one device pixel off; the interior is exact (a separate test asserts exactly that). That is still far better than the old CSS zoom, which would give every other column 3.5 device pixels at 175%.
Frames come from the tick: crew sheet frames are chosen with `idleFrame(tick, fps, count, phase)` inside the fixed step, the same as the enemies' sway, not from Phaser's animation clock. Same tick, same picture; `StageScene.step(n)` runs n ticks without waiting (a test hook that also fits a replay scrubber); hitstop will be "skip the tick". The idle animations are still registered (sheet frame list and fps) for the one-shot poses of the battle test.
Edit seams are real: `setEditMode(true)` makes every sprite interactive with a pixel-perfect hit test and draggable; a drag snaps the feet to the nearest depth row (`snapSlot`, pure and unit-tested) and keeps each side on its half of the screen; depth, shadow and row tint follow. An e2e test drags Kit with the real mouse onto another row and checks the slot, depth and `currentStage()`. Shadow textures are pruned when unused (`pruneShadows`), so a shadow-size slider cannot grow the texture list (tested over 30 slider steps). The `as unknown as HTMLImageElement` cast is gone: stand-in sheets are added as a canvas texture with frames cut by `texture.add`.
Row tint is WebGL-only (Phaser 4's `setTint`): on the canvas renderer the per-row depth tint is not drawn. `window.__stagelab.rowTints` says which, and both canvas-fallback specs assert it is false there; baking per-row texture variants was not worth it for a fallback path.
Split: `boot.ts` (starts the game and scene, reusable by an editor page), `labhook.ts` (the `window.__stagelab` test hook), `status.ts` (the status line), `lab.ts` (about 30 lines that wire them).
CI: the four stagelab specs are in `.github/workflows/ci.yml`'s explicit list. Checked here on Playwright's own Chromium build (revision 1234, Chromium 151, headless shell; not the 1243 this Playwright wants, because installing that is a download I did not make; real CI installs 1243): WebGL there is SwiftShader (software Vulkan, WebGL 1.0), Phaser AUTO picks WEBGL, and all 21 stagelab tests that run pass both with Mark's folder and with `STAGELAB_NO_SPRITES=1` (every request for his folder answered with the app's page, which is what CI will see). Finding: for an unknown path Vite answers 200 text/html, not 404, so `fetchSheetMetas` now also treats a non-JSON answer as missing (before, it only worked because the JSON parse threw), and a test pins it. Finding: `test.use({ launchOptions })` replaces the project's launch options (the no-WebGL spec lost the browser path on this setup), so that spec now switches WebGL off from inside the page instead (an init script makes every WebGL `getContext` answer null). Not done: a run on the exact revision 1243 or on a real CI runner; the first run of PR #4's checks after this push is that proof.
Tests: 384 unit tests (was 367): the new `tests/stagedata.test.ts` (lineup, fight, enemy scale, floor clamp, drag snap, `idleFrame`) and `tests/stagezoom.test.ts`; stagelab e2e is now 22 tests across four files (`stagelab`, `stagelab-nogl`, `stagelab-edit`, `stagelab-dpr`), one of them (the judges' pictures) skipped unless `STAGELAB_MEDIA` is set. `npm run check` exits 0.
Numbers (this machine): Edge on the RTX 4070: first frame 326 ms after navigation, 301 frames in 5 s, interval p50 16.7 / p95 16.9 ms, CPU work per frame p50 0.3 / p95 0.6 ms. Playwright Chromium 151 on SwiftShader (software GL): first frame 300-324 ms, 300 frames, interval p95 16.8 ms, CPU work p95 0.3-0.5 ms (the GL work itself runs on another thread, so that number does not show the software renderer's cost). The test build of the lab: 1,482.8 kB raw and 393.1 kB gzip (Phaser 1,374.5 kB raw / 355.8 kB gzip; the lab's own code and the art generators it reuses 108.3 kB / 37.2 kB, up 9 kB from round 1 for the boot, hook, edit and backdrop code).

## P2-stage notes: the final stage design in Phaser (2026-10-02, round 1)
What was built: `/stagelab.html` now draws the FINAL side-view stage design (`docs/spikes/side-battle-stage.md`) for street and sewer from the final `StageConfig`: the painted 3/4 floor, five depth rows, the party in ref 1's column, enemy slot sets for 1 to 6 enemies and for a boss with 0, 1 or 2 helpers, contact shadows, depth sorting with a figure as one unit, and the whole HUD as Phaser objects with regions from the config, shown in three moments of a turn (choosing, aiming, acting). The page has three pickers (stage, enemy group, moment; also `?stage=sewer&set=boss&phase=act`), `H` hides them and `?clean` leaves them out.
Pictures (`media/spike-phaser/`, git-ignored): `p2-r1-street-lineup-2x.png`, `p2-r1-street-boss-2x.png`, `p2-r1-sewer-lineup-2x.png`, `p2-r1-acting-2x.png` (Rook's Arc Cut on the Warden), `p2-r1-sewer-acting-2x.png` (Kit's Iron Palm), `p2-r1-street-six-2x.png`, and `p2-r1-compare-*.png` with the matching final mockup on top and the Phaser stage under it.
Schema migration: `src/data/stages.json` now holds the two final configs exactly as in the design doc (`docs/spikes/stage-configs/`) plus a `demo` block that is the lab's own (who is in the party and with what gear, which enemies fill each group size, the seed of the example round, and the featured action). `src/stage/config.ts` has the final types and a checker in plain words (one problem per line), `checkLayout` (the design's layout rules: horizon 92 to 112, rows 14 to 24 apart, a light always-on HUD, room above the bottom band) and `checkFigures` (the rules that need sprite sizes: lane, edges, top band). Removed from the schema because the design says so: `enemyScale` (enemy size belongs to the enemy's art), per-row `tint` (one `depthTint` block), `floor.top`/`floor.bottom` (now `floor.y0`, which equals the horizon, and `floor.y1`).
Floor and wall: `src/stage/floor.ts` is a port of the mockup script's floor painter, pure and deterministic (a seeded generator): kerb, bands growing 1.2x, speckle flecks, band seams that strengthen toward the camera, staggered paving joints that meet 400 px above the screen, lane seams, the dashed amber lane line, neon spill and streaks from the signs, puddles kept clear of every slot, haze. `src/stage/sewerwall.ts` ports the sewer's replaced back wall (planks, pillars, copper pipe, lamps, the green-lit grate, the water channel). The street's skyline is the game's own backdrop slid up by `shiftY` with the dithered sky fade (`reprojectWall`). The result is baked into ONE 480x270 texture named after a fingerprint of the config that made it (`stagePictureKey`); only the current stage's picture is kept, so a dragged horizon never leaves one per step. Checked: the wall rows of the Phaser stage have the same mean colours as the mockup's in six regions; the floor differs only where the seeded generator differs (flecks and puddle spots: Python's generator cannot be matched, the look is the same).
Figures: each fighter is a record with its parts (body sprite, contact shadow, ring under the feet, a dotted "home" ring, an enemy's health bar) and `restyle` places them all from the one depth number plus a fixed fraction (`PART`: shadow -0.5, ring -0.4, body 0, smear +0.1, bar +0.25); rows are 1000 apart, so a nearer fighter covers every part of a farther one, health bar included (the design's known gap). Ties on one row follow the design: further from the middle first, heroes before enemies. A lunging attacker sorts by the target's row plus 1 (`sortRow`). Shadow width follows the sprite's drawn width within the stage's limits (a boss has its own); the acting hero gets a cyan ring and the target an amber one.
Depth haze: the design's per-row blend toward a fog colour is baked as a copy of each sprite's texture (`hazedTexture`, once per sprite and amount, found again by name), not Phaser's multiply tint, so it works on the canvas renderer too (the round-2 note that the row tint was WebGL-only no longer applies). The hit flash is a baked copy too (`flashTexture`: near-white, dark outline kept), and the acting hero and its target are exempt from haze.
HUD: `src/stage/hud.ts` builds each region as a Phaser Container at the config's box (window frame image, text images, face chips, icons, and one Graphics for bars), shown by the config's `show` rule (`hudlayout.ts`, pure): top turn timeline (NOW chip, heroes above the line, enemies below with a pink corner mark), party table bottom-left (face, name, HP bar, HP now/max, resource), command icons bottom-centre, enemy box bottom-right (foe list while choosing, target details while aiming or acting), the skill banner and the combo counter. Text uses the game's own bitmap font (`drawText` onto small canvases that become textures), window frames are the game's `drawWindow` look, the five command icons are the mockup's placeholders written out as text (`icons.ts`), faces are cut from the sprites on the stage (`faces.ts`: grain-1 heroes cut 1:1, the shipped grain-2 enemies cut at double size and shrunk by exactly 2). Over the stage: the acting hero's name tab, the aimed enemy's name tab with its A/B letter, and the damage number (CRIT over it when critical; beside the upper body of a tall target, kept on the screen).
Real numbers: `src/stage/demo.ts` builds the HUD's view from the game's own code.
